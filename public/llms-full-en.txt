# CHAT GAME — Detailed version

Summary version: /llms-en.txt
Japanese version: /llms-full.txt

## Overview
CHAT GAME collects prompts that turn a general AI chat assistant into a
playable game. No install, login, or API key is needed. Every game page exposes
the full prompt with a one-click copy button, and the layout is mobile-first so
it works on a phone as well as a desktop browser.

## Languages
Languages: Japanese, English, Korean, Chinese, Spanish, Portuguese (the prompts themselves are translated too)
Locales are ja, en, ko, zh, es, pt, and URLs follow /{locale}/...
A URL without a locale prefix is routed from the browser's Accept-Language
header, falling back to English when no supported language matches.
Every page carries hreflang links to all six versions.

## Site structure
- /{locale} (home): introduction, three-step usage guide, card list of every game with inline copy buttons
- /{locale}/how-to-play: the three steps, recovering when the AI forgets the rules, what the prompts have in common, FAQ
- /{locale}/games/{slug} (game detail): description, full prompt with copy button, how to play, strategy tips
- /llms.txt, /llms-full.txt, /llms-en.txt, /llms-full-en.txt: information for AI agents
- /robots.txt, /sitemap.xml (6 locales x 9 pages = 54 URLs)

## Games

### Number Guessing (/{locale}/games/number-guess)
Category: deduction / Difficulty: hard / About 20-40 min / 1 player
The AI acts as game master for two NPCs, each holding a secret number that must
satisfy: at least 100, below 100,000,000, divisible by 100, and containing at
most two non-zero digits in total. The player asks each NPC up to five questions
(ten in total). Both mathematical questions and subjective questions about the
size of the amount are allowed, and the hesitation in the AI's phrasing is an
intentional, legitimate hint.

### Whodunit (/{locale}/games/whodunit)
Category: deduction / Difficulty: normal / About 15-30 min / 1 player
The AI generates a minor crime and three suspects. The two innocent suspects
always tell the truth; only the culprit lies, and only about facts that
incriminate them. The player may ask each suspect up to three questions (nine in
total). The case is required to be solvable purely by cross-checking times and
locations.

### Spot the Liar (/{locale}/games/find-the-liar)
Category: deduction / Difficulty: hard / About 15-30 min / 1 player
The AI sets up a trivial incident (someone ate the last cake) and three
characters, A, B and C. Two facts are decided independently: who caused the
incident, and who is the liar. They may or may not be the same person. The liar
inverts the truth of every statement, including statements about themselves; the
other two always tell the truth. The player has seven questions, counted by
proposition rather than by sentence, so asking about two people at once costs
two. Winning requires naming both the culprit and the liar.

### Word Wolf, solo edition (/{locale}/games/word-wolf)
Category: hidden role / Difficulty: normal / About 10-20 min / 1 player
A social deduction game adapted for solo play: the AI performs four characters
at once. Three share a secret word and one holds a different one. Across three
rounds of conversation the player may question one character per round, then
names the odd one out.

### Escape Room (/{locale}/games/escape-room)
Category: adventure / Difficulty: normal / About 20-40 min / 1 player
A text adventure. The AI fixes the room design (objects, items, lock mechanism,
solution path) up front and must not alter it mid-game. The player has 30 turns.
Every obtainable item must be used somewhere in the solution, so no red herrings
are introduced.

### Haggle (/{locale}/games/negotiation)
Category: simulation / Difficulty: normal / About 10-20 min / 1 player
The AI plays a merchant holding a hidden reserve price set between 55% and 75%
of the opening price. The player negotiates for ten turns. Repeatedly demanding
far below the reserve breaks off the deal. At the end the reserve price is
revealed and the discount achieved is scored out of 100.

### 20 Questions (/{locale}/games/twenty-questions)
Category: deduction / Difficulty: easy / About 5-10 min / 1 player
The AI picks a common noun and answers up to twenty yes/no questions, displaying
the remaining question count after every reply. The lightest entry point on the
site.

## Shared prompt-design principles

Verified present in all seven prompts:
1. A list of prohibitions - what the AI must not do, written out explicitly.
2. The hidden information (answer, secret word, culprit, reserve price) is fixed
   before play begins and may not change. Inventing it after seeing the player's
   guess is forbidden too.
3. The answer is revealed at the end.

Present in some but not all:
- Reporting the remaining turns or questions every reply (Word Wolf is
  round-based and phrases this differently).
- Refusing to dodge an ambiguous question (absent from Escape Room, Haggle and
  Word Wolf).
- A short debrief of what worked.

These rules exist to stop the model from retroactively rationalising its earlier
answers, the most common failure mode for conversational games. They are
instructions, not guarantees: a model holds nothing but the conversation itself.

## FAQ
Q. Which assistants work?
A. Any chat assistant that accepts a long prompt, such as ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.

Q. Is an account required?
A. Not for this site. The terms of the AI service you paste into still apply.

Q. The AI forgets the rules mid-game.
A. Replying "follow the rules from the original instructions" usually restores it.

Q. May I modify the prompts?
A. Yes. They are written to be edited, for example to change the turn limit.
